## Deep Dark Trenches

It is one late afternoon in February 2024, I find myself in the office of my psychologist which had no windows.
Tears fill my eyes and roll down my cheeks, while he discusses the severity of my condition with my father who came as well to the meeting to understand more about what I was going through. I was diagnosed with severe anxiety.
For the past few months I haven't been able to function normally in my day-to-day life.
I had a high-functioning anxiety that followed me every minute of every day, and manifested as a persistent sense of some large machine "crushing" my consciousness. It was a very painful, mentally.

> [The anxiety] followed me every minute of every day, and manifested as a persistent sense of some large machine "crushing" my consciousness. It was a very painful, mentally.

I was barely holding myself together in front of people, and I couldn't imagine how my future would look like.
I was trapped in my head as if I was watching life around me unroll as a movie.
Then, there are my thoughts. Fast, reactive, fearful, and spinning out of control, revolving around how bad my situation was.
My world vision was distorted. I was flooded with negativity and fear, without any external reason.
I heard my psychologist say that the recovery process would take between one and a half and three years, but I had to
change my solitary, delusional lifestyle. So in that moment I made a promise to myself - that no matter how bad my current situation was, I'm going to get out of it.
I'm going to do whatever was necessary to get myself out of the deep dark trenches I had fallen into.
I gripped onto faith, rolled my sleeves, and I started walking towards the light at the end of the tunnel, which I couldn't yet see.

## The Recovery Process

I started my recovery process the very moment, I left the meeting of my therapist.
There were many things I experimented with on my healing journey, so I will outline here what helped me most.
There isn't a particular order to them.

### Faith

At the beginning, the intensity of my anxiety was so high that I just couldn't imagine how it would get better.
So what I did was I had faith. I trusted the healing process and that things are going to improve over time, despite all the
ruminating thoughts in my head that would tell me otherwise. I also started nurturing a new life philosophy - that life is a miracle, no matter what happens,
and every experience is intrinsically something pleasant, merely because it is part of life.

> Life is a miracle, no matter what happens, and every experience is intrinsically something pleasant, merely because it is part of life.

That faith and the new perspective brought me a tiny sense of relief. I trusted deeply that no matter how terrible I felt, there was a way out.

### Serving others

Back in 2024, I felt at a very low stage in my life. I had a sense that everything I did to improve my headspace wasn't working (even though I knew on a conscious level that it should work).
So I felt the need to help others around me. At the moment where I felt I couldn't help myself, I had an unexplicable desire to help others around me - to serve them.

> At the moment where I felt I couldn't help myself, I had an unexplicable desire to help others around me - to serve them.

And this is what I did. I got a job at the nearest McDonalds in my neighbourhood, and I worked there half time during the summer of 2024. Fulfilling orders of clients gave me a sense of contribution.
Paradoxically, my suffering lessened as a result of serving something other than myself.

### Dancing

Back in time when I felt severity anxious, I also felt very lonely, isolated from society and from fellow human beings.
I missed love, I missed connection in my life.
So I felt the need to sign up for dancing classes again (as I already knew about their existence from before) and that was what I did.
I started attending Salsa & Bachata lessons twice a week or more, and during the weekends I was regularly going to latin parties one of Sofia's latin hot-spots - Club Caliente, in the city center.
In the months that followed I made new friends, I laughed a lot, I fell in love, I learned to care about other people, and I discovered how foundational human connection is for one's own mental wellbeing.

> I learned to care about other people, and I discovered how foundational human connection is for one's own mental wellbeing.

Latin dancing brought light to the darkness I was experiencing. It helped me rediscover that life had moments of joy, and I just had to open my heart and mind to them.

### Focusing on the future, not the past

When a person is experiencing great pain psychologically, there is a tendency to start searching for its cause in the past. The mind thinks that once you find it, it would point you to a solution to the problem.
But this can lead to a vicious cycle where you always dwell on the past searching for causes whenever you experience anxiety. You become stuck.
Instead, I thought it was a much better idea to focus on creating a desirable future, the one I want to have, and focus on taking the necessary actions in the present moment, today.
This made an impact on my mental state for the day. Why? Since my focus was not on my anxiety and the rumination in my mind, but rather on some positive aspect of my future.
I took driving lessons and got my driving license. I imagined myself as a great latin dancer and who went to festivals and got attention with his great dance style and vibes.
I worked out which brings me to the next item on the list - Sport.

### Sport

Sport helped me tremendously over the past 2 years in my healing process. I tried many different sports, including, but not limited to: Dancing, Running, Calisthenics, Gym,
Pilates, Yoga, Feminine Hip-Hop, and more. However, what stuck with me was Calisthenics and Running. Calisthenics in the gym in the Netherlands was a way for me to get out of my head and into my body.
When the pain of an exercise hit me, my mind couldn't focus on what's wrong in my life, it focused on the present moment difficulty of the exercise. So over these two hours the "tight knot" of anxiety inside my head, was almost unnoticable.
I am really thankful that I used sport in those moments, instead of running for alcohol or some other destructive addiction. I was being antifragile, although I didn't know it at the time. Antifragility is a term popularized by famous writer Nassim Taleb.

> To be antifragile means to grow stronger, instead of breaking down, when exposed to stressors.

Someone else in my position might have become an alcoholic, by reaching for alcohol as a way out, I got stronger, physically, by reaching for the pull up bar. This allowed me to regain 10+ kg muscle mass, despite having an active autoimmune condition.

### Meditation

Here it comes, the habit everyone is talking about nowadays - meditation.
The way I practiced meditation was by sitting or lying down in a quiet space with eyes closed and observing what thoughts come to my mind, without chasing after them. I was just noticing my thoughts.
I got to say it was extremely painful and difficult to do this practice. The reason is that meditation eliminates external distractions, allowing the thoughts and sensations to fill in that space.
Since I was having anxious thoughts, it was like watching a horror movie unroll in my head and I was the central character of that movie. It was very unpleasant.
However, the evidence behind the benefits of meditation was very solid. So I trusted it and I did the practice.
The way meditation helped me in this period was by giving me the opportunity to face my anxiety head-on, and it showed myself that I could endure just sitting with the unpleasant thoughts and feelings.
This created some breathing room between me and my anxiety. It allowed me to live in parallel with my anxiety without being so entangled in it.

### Great sleep

Regular high-quality sleep has been proved to improve mental health. I identified myself as a professional sleep athlete, similarly to Bryan Johnson.
Since it was difficult to fall asleep I learned and applied a plethora of science-backed techniques to fall asleep faster and improve the quality of my sleep.
I aimed to get to bed around 22:30-23, and avoided my phone an hour before my bedtime. I used a diffuser with lavander and pine essential oils to calm the nervous system down and bring the scent of nature in my bedroom
to relax further. I had my last meal at least 3-4 hours before bed in order to get into that restorative deep sleep in the first half of the night.
Everything I did during the day as a way to improve my mental health was only a signal to my nervous system to change. The actual change and improvement happened during sleep. That was when
the nervous system rewired itself, based on what I did during the day. As I held onto faith, served others, danced, focused on the future and not the past, practiced meditation, and didn't give as much attention to my anxious thoughts, the brain connections
for anxiety got weaker, day after day, while the connections for calmness, peace, and joy grew stronger. Over the months, sleep was what made the actual positive changes happen.

## Where I Stand Today

As I'm writing this, it is already June 2026, two years and four months after that meeting with my therapist in his office. My anxiety has improved to the point where I have a general peace of mind during my day, and I can engage in new activities without panic and fear overwhelming me, but don't get me wrong - I still experience regular moments of anxiety.
However, compared to two years ago, it is 10 times less intense and much less often. I still feel a resonance in my mind of the past. My mind is still more prone that my peers to jump into worst case scenarios and make quick negative conclusions.
I understand that and accept it as part of me at the current stage in my life. I know I'm changing it with the daily work I put in.
I still aim to get regular high-quality sleep, I meditate once or twice a day for 20 minutes, and I have "micro-meditations" throughout my whole day that last a few seconds. I still dance salsa and bachata (I love bachata more!), recently, trying out kizomba, too.
I don't think so much about myself and my problems or achievements, but I instead ask myself, "How can I contribute to society here in Bulgaria and the world?".
I take bold decisions to engage in new activities and go to place, I have never been before. The work is still going, but I'm fine with that. I feel excited about the future and the great things that will happen to me in ways I can not imagine now.
